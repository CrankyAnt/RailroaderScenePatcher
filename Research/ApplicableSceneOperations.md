# Applicable scene-object operations

Evidence for [Establish missing evidence for applicable scene-object operations](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/issues/18), [Choose the initial scene-object and operation support boundary](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/issues/7#issuecomment-6715003887) and [Define the first usable Railroader Scene Patcher](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/issues/3). Untested operations remain in scope; no production support follows from this table.

## Evidence identity

Repository text uses LF line endings. [Text provenance](TextProvenance.json) records original and retained hashes and the original CRLF line positions for normalized files, so captured input bytes can be reconstructed. Binary and complete runtime-log hashes identify the original captures; text normalization does not change those records.

The prior audit identified Railroader 0.6.2 experimental `Railroader.dll` SHA-256 `8E9ACFD583C1DA79655C970773D42F2179E999F62DC36A3C6BC3A7B94C27A91E`. This hash does not identify all assets. The [native capability audit](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/00940975c8f1193928bad5f6aca1ff44e13f3f7b/Research/NativeCapabilityAudit.md) is static evidence; the [operations report](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/3db820db8e34b5dc98b6df4c4ffa376963bfa440/Research/SceneOperations.md) records the fixture, build hashes, methods, observations and limitations for the prior runtime cases. The [lifecycle report](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/0f8d3fb69cc362c5a56a22910bdb5a93aa75acc3/Research/SceneLifecycle.md) distinguishes static registration, placement and group-subscription paths. ## Remaining ownership and consumer distinctions

Read-only Mono.Cecil inspection of the installed `Railroader.dll` identified the following code paths. They are static findings for the assembly hash above, not proof of runtime equivalence or complete coverage. The [original inventory](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/4c00445b048adccb6920c238116d1f7e1e915db9/Research/Scope/ScopeInventory.md), [candidate families](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/4c00445b048adccb6920c238116d1f7e1e915db9/Research/Scope/CandidateFamilies.md) and [reference families](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/4c00445b048adccb6920c238116d1f7e1e915db9/Research/Scope/GameReferenceFamilies.md) identify the original candidates and owner evidence.

- `TrackMarker.OnEnable` registers a marker ID, type and object with the graph. The earlier batch observed broken speed Mwp1, yard-limit Mkwd, whistle Mm8w and station-sign Mi4f. The installed enum contains Generic, Signal, Flare, Crossing and PassengerStop; descriptive sign names do not establish an instance's enum. `Graph.EnumerateTrackMarkers` is queried by inspected AI planner paths for signal, flare, crossing and passenger-stop categories. The planner's speed calculation reads track segment properties, which does not establish a scene speed sign's effect. Shared registration does not establish the downstream consumers or equivalence of the named instances.
- `NativeTrack.GetMarkerLocation` first queries the graph by ID and can fall back to loaded marker objects, including inactive objects. A successful script lookup therefore does not by itself prove a graph registration. Actual scripts using these particular IDs remain unidentified.
- The scene crossing pad Mg74 has `TrackCurveMeshBuilder` as well as marker and track-relative components. `TrackCurveMeshBuilder.GetCurves` reads the marker location and derives graph curves; `CurveMeshBuilderBase.OnEnable` calls `Generate`, which replaces generated container children and creates renderers with a culling token. Automatic regeneration, colliders, culling and restoration after rebind remain untested.
- `IndustryDataLoader.CreatePassengerStop` resolves map-data span references for a `PassengerStop`; `PassengerStop.FindCars` uses `TrainController.CarsOnSpan` and filters stopped passenger cars. `StationAgent.ResolveReferences` separately looks up a passenger stop by ID in `OpsController.AllPassengerStops`. No affected scene-owned passenger-stop instance or required scene repair has been established. The Wilmot span failure in the earlier batch involved native operations data.
- The Sox water-column anchor was repaired in map JSON for that case. `BuiltinBuilder.DisableUnresolvedTrackMarkers` is a distinct code path and does not establish that every anchor has the same owner or that its consumers remain correct after disable.
- The Ela bridge and construction scenery are separate roots. Bridge pose and activity observations do not establish collision contact or construction and progression behavior. The independent Sylva board has no marker in its inspected hierarchy; its mask, culling and floating-origin effects remain open.
- CTC, signal and interlocking relationships are included in the historical inventory but excluded by the recorded product boundary. This does not remove other untested candidates from scope.

## Bridge root experiment

The operator used the same build in the editor with a fixed logged origin, captured the Ela bridge, moved the root 5 m in world X and rotated it 15 degrees around world Y, repeated the move, and restored. A fresh capture preceded a separate root disable and restore. No editor save was made. The first round was remote from the camera. After the camera was positioned at the bridge and the origin settled, the operator repeated the sequence with a fresh capture. Screenshots show the steel bridge and concrete piers move together while the track and right-hand timber approach remain in place; the bridge returns visually after restoration. Disabling the root hides the steel bridge and piers while the track and timber approach remain visible, and restoration makes the bridge visible again. No train or collision-contact test was performed.

Each [selected snapshot](Evidence/BridgeBoot03.txt) in the first round contains 28 hierarchy nodes and seven collider records. The two move snapshots have the same logged root pose. A line comparison of the first baseline and post-move restore found 30 different node lines and two different collider lines, paired before and after; the root local X differed by 0.0005 and several child world X values differed by approximately 0.001 at logged precision. The separate baseline and post-disable restore matched all logged node and collider lines. Collider bounds were formatted as rounded strings. These observations do not establish bit-exact restoration, physical contact, culler state or behavior on reload. Full source-log SHA-256 after both rounds was `19CDA222FB12A9C13AA7B3384DDD739EA0BCB9651DE7DFDECCEE5EF97CE63CA4`.

The exact source snapshot for the E094 bridge build was not retained. The later `ApplicableProbe` source retains the bridge mutation methods but also adds other controls and inspection code; it is not a byte-identical build record.

## Decisions still requiring evidence

- Authoring and native coverage depend on the remaining map-editor sign routes, Resume and deletion, actual retained-object selection and the independent board's mask, culler and origin behavior. One unsuccessful click does not decide every route.
- Patch-document identity and compatibility depend on the applicable marker types, owners and consumer links and on resolution across the intended builds and layers. A scene path, marker ID or successful same-build registration is not a cross-version guarantee.
- Application and failure behavior depend on ordinary-save and editor lifecycle differences, automatic reapplication beyond the recorded marker fixture, restoration of affected subscriptions and consumers, and a verified treatment of resolved warnings.
- The named broken crossing and its restored endpoint have now been exercised with one locomotive in both directions. Physical light flashes and audible bell, other routes and instances, and reload behavior remain outside those observations; controller signal values are not substitutes for physical observations.
- Placement and restoration depend on a populated milepost origin cache across changes and rendered display, board mask/culler/origin behavior, bridge collision and construction dependencies, and applicable group behavior beyond the recorded callback sequence. The bridge roundtrip also showed numeric differences at logged precision.
- The named industry, passenger, built-in and anchor cases have the ownership dispositions above. An unidentified scene-only instance remains unknown, not a reason to invent an additional fixture or assume all related objects are natively editable.

The bounded editor and ordinary-save experiments, broken-crossing trial, original-instance preflight and evidence-reuse limits are recorded above for the dependent design decisions. Unresolved details, including arbitrary origin shifts, subscriptions, physical light flashes and audible bell, remain findings for design and later validation. Cross-version guarantees, every collector configuration and exhaustive native menu paths are not implied by these observations. None changes the recorded capability-based product boundary or constitutes a general production support claim.
