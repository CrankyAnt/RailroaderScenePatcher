# Ownership and scene-load readiness

Research for [Test writable map ownership and scene-load readiness](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/issues/15), serving [Define the first usable Railroader Scene Patcher](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/issues/3).

## Build, fixture and method

The installed `Railroader.dll` SHA-256 measured during preparation was `8e9acfd583c1da79655c970773d42f2179e999f62dc36a3c6bc3a7b94c27a91e`. The orchestrator reports Railroader 0.6.2 build 26335745, a separate `-homedir` and Player.log, and six enabled fixture and probe packs applied together after startup. The [probe and fixture source](OwnershipProbe/) compiled without warnings or errors. The orchestrator opened A and B in the editor and exited without saving changes, then created two disposable ordinary saves selecting B. The first was contaminated by another fixture and is not clean evidence. The second was created after disabling that fixture. After a full restart the clean save was loaded, saved and quit, and reloaded in the same process. It was saved and quit again after the second observation.

The evidence includes `C:\TMP\RailroaderMapResearch\Observations\Boot01Ownership.log`, `Boot01EditorProbe.log`, `Boot02Ownership.log`, `Boot03Ownership.log` and `Boot03SceneProbe.log`. The ownership logs retain original Player.log line numbers and path classes. Scoped [Boot02](OwnershipProbe/Boot02Excerpt.txt), [Boot03 ownership](OwnershipProbe/Boot03OwnershipExcerpt.txt) and [Boot03 scene](OwnershipProbe/Boot03SceneExcerpt.txt) excerpts preserve relevant rows without full Player logs. References identify the boot and original line numbers. All observations are specific to these runs; path classes do not expose exact paths.

The selected A definition, its owner, and the map-data source coincide in this fixture. Its layer source differs. B's presence and writable flag did not make it the active edit target. These rows support investigating the active definition and owner lookup as candidate inputs to choosing a destination; they do not show a patch write, prove the destination is correct in other configurations, or establish which loaded packs are relevant. A dependency or presence in `OrderedPacks` does not establish discovery of arbitrary JSON.

For B, the reported layer identifiers and resolved data roots show the cross-pack A and game-core sources in this fixture. The orchestrator reports that all probes and A/B were enabled. The initial ordinary save had seven marker errors because `EditorFixture14/Scripts/Data.rl` unconditionally merges into ordinary maps. After disabling only that fixture, the orchestrator created a new clean sandbox; no new marker warnings were observed on its load. The contaminated save is excluded from clean conclusions. The orchestrator also reports a read-only SceneProbe snapshot in the clean ordinary world that resolved one M5pp Shuo and one independent board as active, and an EditorProbe snapshot with no ordinary-world controller and its diagnostic UI hidden. These are separately reported observations, not independently reproduced by this probe.

Boot02 recorded B's `GameUI`, `MapCore`, `BushnellWhittier-MapManager` and `Environment-Enviro` loads before `OnWorldLoad` in both the editor and clean ordinary route (lines 3539-4096 and 23030-23574). At each B world-load snapshot the global marker/relative-position/span counts were 388/370/357 (lines 4207-4209 and 23684-23686). In the editor, those scenes unloaded before `OnWorldUnload`, where the active identifier was null and counts were 1/0/0 (lines 8523-9091). The contaminated ordinary save also unloaded (lines 20329-20897); its later clean replacement is a new save, not a re-entry test. The counts and sampled objects do not establish universal readiness or mutation safety.

On Boot03 both loads of the same clean save reported B as the definition, `fixture-B` as owner and data source, A and core as layer sources, and `IsEditTarget=False`. Both `OnWorldLoad` snapshots reported global counts 388/370/357; both unloads reported a null identifier and counts 1/0/0 (lines 1502-1614, 2644-2755, 3979-4091 and 5904-6015). The scene excerpt records one M5pp marker with matching Shuo registration and one active independent board on each entry. These snapshots occurred after each world load. The later component and root disable-and-restore operations occurred after the second snapshot and are not evidence of initial lifecycle availability. The orchestrator reports restoration before save and no marker warnings. This establishes the sampled observations for this save and build, not readiness of every object or a safe mutation point.

For A, a periodic snapshot already reported the active definition and edit flag before `sceneLoaded GameUI` (lines 3204-3209, 3317-3323). The probe then observed `GameUI`, `MapCore`, `BushnellWhittier-MapManager`, and `Environment-Enviro` scene loads, followed by `OnWorldLoad` (lines 3317, 3432, 3549, 3666, 3874). The three global counts for `TrackMarker`, `TrackRelativePosition`, and `TrackSpan` were 1/0/0 at GameUI, 362/344/161 at the map-manager scene, and 388/370/357 at environment load and `OnWorldLoad` (lines 3428-3430, 3660-3662, 3777-3779, 3985-3987). A later periodic snapshot reported the same counts (lines 4110-4113). These are `Resources.FindObjectsOfTypeAll` counts, not proof that each object belongs to the active scene, is ready for mutation, or is safely registered.

During exit, `Environment-Enviro` unloaded while the counts were still 388/370/357. After `BushnellWhittier-MapManager` unloaded they were 1/0/0, although the active A definition and edit flag still appeared. At `OnWorldUnload` the active definition was null and counts were 1/0/0 (lines 5360-5473, 5474-5587, 5816-5928). Thus the edit flag alone did not indicate object availability during teardown. The remaining marker is a global count and cannot be attributed to the unloaded map. The separate editor-probe log reports a controller attachment during the A session and its detachment on exit; it does not establish object readiness.

## Limits and decision inputs

- The probe logs store writability and classified roots, but does not execute or verify a candidate write, nor identify an authored-patch destination independently of the definition owner. It does not demonstrate a runtime discovery route.
- The Boot01 and Boot02 probe returns null both for a missing reflected member and a legitimate null value. Boot03 used the corrected probe, DLL SHA-256 `01f04108d2d6bd1514ca2ad5a5eb3d44668c1d960d7f98cf3adb4826fc7008da`, which reports missing members and access errors separately. Its outer catch can still terminate the rest of a snapshot. The ownership probe does not log object identity, scene ownership, active state or registration, so aggregate counts cannot establish representative object readiness or persistence.
- The snapshot repeats all packs and counts every ten seconds and has no explicit test-step markers. Preserve original line numbers and record operator transitions when comparing runs. The separate editor-probe timestamps and Player.log row order have different log formats; do not assume exact cross-log callback ordering without a common marker.

The [previous static ownership report](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/c7f2ce79ef1ef813d62ee78414f26a7eeb1b84ae/Research/MapModPatchLoading.md) identifies internal candidates, not a supported extension contract. For the patch document and target-identity decision, the active edit definition plus its owner and writable store are candidate inputs, with explicit handling required for missing or ambiguous values. For the authoring-experience decision, edit mode and scene availability must be considered separately, especially during teardown. For compatibility and dependency decisions, pack activation, map-layer resolution and arbitrary patch-file discovery are separate questions. No actual patch write, general discovery mechanism or safe mutation point is demonstrated.
