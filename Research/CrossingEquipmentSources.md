## Equipment and addressing

The preflight found two original outer signal roots, `Signal 1` and `Signal 2`, on the controller. Each contained a `Track.KeyValueLampMaterialController` with key `lights` targeting its own `Crossing Signal Wishbone A (1)` model. The model contained an `Animator` and `RollingStock.Controls.KeyValueBoolAnimator` with key `gates` and a `Scene` animation clip. The controller's `Helpers.Culling.GameObjectCuller` listed the two original signal roots. The retained report identifies this one combined signal and gate model, not a complete inventory of crossing equipment families, standalone light models, gates or alternate source assets. [Source: retained equipment draft, Scene inspection.]

`Track.Crossings.GradeCrossingController.OnEnableWithProperties` obtains a `KeyValue.Runtime.KeyValueObject` on its own object and uses `lights`, `gates` and `bell` values. The inspected controller metadata had no serialized device list. The lamp and gate animator search their parent hierarchy for a `KeyValueObject`, use their configured key, and register observers. Thus, placing an otherwise valid device under the intended controller is a plausible addressing route, provided no nearer key-value owner intercepts the lookup. It is an inference from the recorded code paths, not proof of initialization or operation in a newly instantiated object. [Source: retained equipment draft, Static findings.]

The lamp discovers renderers and retains material property blocks. The gate animator needs an `Animator` and its assigned animation clip and creates a playable. Both inherit `Game.GameBehaviour`, whose `OnEnable` registers initialization with `Game.State.RestoreNotifier`. The recorded disable paths dispose observers, and the animator destroys its playable on destruction. A clone enabled after world restoration needs a runtime check of observer registration and both initial and later state changes. [Source: retained equipment draft, Static findings.]

The controller itself creates a `Crossing Bell` child with `Audio.IntegerLoopingPlayer`, loads an audio reference and starts a coroutine. Its recorded default identifier is `rr/bell-uss-teardrop-crossing-rh`; disable stops the coroutine, cancels the load, destroys the player and disposes the loaded reference. The preflight found a generated bell and audio-source children. Copying the signal therefore does not create a second controlled bell emitter. That historical preflight did not establish audio asset availability or a separately added emitter. The later completed trial establishes the loaded asset and second player within its recorded limits; isolated added-emitter audibility, attenuation guarantees and independent asset lifetime remain unproven. [Source: retained equipment draft, Static findings and Scene inspection.]

## Construction and verification

The retained helper proposes cloning the complete first outer signal into an initially inactive wrapper parented under the controller. It checks the clone's lamp target points to the cloned model, the gate has an animator and the same non-null clip asset, and no controller, span or key-value owner occurs inside the source hierarchy. It records original controller, source, culler and span references before enabling the wrapper. These are useful trial guards, not evidence that Unity's reference remapping or initialization succeeded in a run. It leaves the added object outside the native culler's original collection, so its visibility and lifecycle differ unless separately addressed. [Source: retained trial helper, `Device`, `Add`, `OriginalIntact`.]

No retained source establishes the copied hierarchy's collider types or whether it has collision contact. Inspect colliders and layer settings before and after the clone and test actual contact if collision matters. Copying a visual object does not by itself establish physical collision or safe placement. The inspected source also does not establish a general native authoring or persistence route for added equipment. The [native capability audit](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/8d3bfc0005d15e8b0404fc584479bd60f385c4fd/Research/NativeCapabilityAudit.md) records a bounded editor and serialization search, not proof that every indirect route is absent.

## Minimum probe

In a clean disposable world, identify exactly one controller and source signal and capture its hierarchy, key-value owner, lamp targets, animation, active states, culler membership, colliders, circuit membership and baseline output. Clone that complete signal under an inactive wrapper attached to the intended controller, at a safe visible offset. Before enabling, verify remapped internal targets and shared external clip, absence of a nearer key-value owner, and preservation of the original source and circuits. Enable it and check observers and initial state. Use the existing circuit with a train to compare original and added lamp material output and gate movement through activation and release. Observe audio separately at both locations; do not infer a second emitter. Check collision and visibility separately where applicable.

Deactivate and remove the clone, wait for deferred destruction, and check observer cleanup, original equipment, circuit references and culler state. Repeat the add operation and test a fresh-world reapplication on the intended load routes. Record the game assembly and asset context, fixture, exact source and controller, poses, outputs, photographs or logs, and failure conditions. The retained helper's plan does not itself demonstrate any of those outcomes.

## Direct assembly inspection

Read-only inspection used `Mono.Cecil.dll` from the installed `coverlet.collector` NuGet package and `AssemblyDefinition.ReadAssembly` on the DLL identified above. Method-body operands and control flow support these version-specific findings:

- `Game.GameBehaviour.OnEnable` queues `OnEnableWithProperties` through `Game.State.RestoreNotifier.RegisterForRestore`. `NotifyDidRestore` processes the queue; after restore reaches `Complete`, `LateUpdate` processes later queued registrations. A newly enabled clone should therefore receive initialization on a subsequent `LateUpdate`, assuming the shared notifier exists and continues updating. This is a static expectation; inspect the actual observer after a frame and after enable cycles.
- `KeyValueLampMaterialController.OnEnableWithProperties` returns when no parent `KeyValueObject` exists. Otherwise it discovers child renderers of configured target objects, matches their shared materials to configured material targets, displays the current Boolean value, then subscribes to the key. `KeyValueBoolAnimator.OnEnableWithProperties` likewise returns without a parent owner. It obtains the local `Animator`, adds the configured clip to its playable adapter, applies the current Boolean value and subscribes. Missing targets, shared material mappings, animator, clip or a nearer owner can prevent the intended result.
- `GameObjectCuller.OnEnable` registers a sphere at its transform and radius. Its `CullingSphereStateChanged` changes active state of objects in its `gameObjects` list. An unlisted clone will not follow that list's state changes. Adding it to the list also requires testing its current culling state and cleanup; the callback and sphere are controller-specific.
- `GradeCrossingController.CreateBellPlayer` creates a child player, sets mixer group `LocomotiveBell`, priority `10`, and `audioDistance` enum value `3`. `LoadBellAudio` resolves the `rr/bell-uss-teardrop-crossing-rh` `BellDefinition` through the train controller's content library, obtains its `IndexedAudioDefinition`, builds an `AssetLocator` and calls `PrefabStoreExtensions.LoadAssetAsync<AudioClip>`. It passes the loaded asset, indexes and volume to `IntegerLoopingPlayer.Configure(AudioClip, float[], float)`. The controller retains and disposes the loaded asset reference and cancels pending loads on disable. Its loop sets the existing player's `play` field from the circuit-derived lights state. A second emitter needs its own player, asset lifetime and an explicit binding to the controller's signal; adding the player alone does not bind it. `IntegerLoopingPlayer.OnEnable` prepares pooled sources and clips, and disable returns sources and destroys owned split clips. This describes a candidate internal API route, not a tested implementation or permission to copy the controller.
- `Model.ComponentBuilderContext.InstantiatePrefab<T>` delegates to `Model.IPrefabInstantiator.InstantiatePrefab<T>`. Component builders use this for rolling-stock components, including `BellComponentBuilder` and `CompressorComponentBuilder`. These are established prefab construction paths in the assembly, but their rolling-stock context does not establish an addressable crossing-equipment prefab or a suitable scene-equipment loader. The existing scene signal remains the concrete source identified here.

### Extra bell player API and lifetime

The controller's `SignalBell`, `SignalLights`, `_bellPlayer`, `_keyValueObject` and `_audioLoadReference` are private. `KeyValue.Runtime.KeyValueObject` exposes a public string indexer and `Observe(string, Action<Value>, bool)` returning `IDisposable`. The installed `KeyValue.Runtime.dll` SHA-256 is `5C6B3BD5AC0B3DE4484E56FC9B379521F1B336B0D04649DC1A8F3CB23937CE53`. An independent player can read the controller object's own key `bell`, subscribe to changes and dispose its observer on removal. It must apply the current value as well as later changes. The controller loop sets this key and its own player from the same computed lights state; it does not discover or drive an added player automatically.

`IntegerLoopingPlayer` exposes public `play`, `volume`, `mixerGroup`, `priority`, `audioDistance`, `randomize` and `indexedClip` fields and `Configure(AudioClip, float[], float)`. The controller sets `LocomotiveBell`, priority `10` and `AudioDistance.Distant` before asynchronous configuration. `BellDefinition.Audio` returns an `IndexedAudioDefinition`, whose `Audio`, `Indexes` and `Volume` properties are public. A separate player can follow the existing definition and loader sequence, call `Configure` with its loaded clip, indexes and volume, set `play` from the controller's key, and own cancellation, observer and loaded-reference cleanup. The player prepares pooled sources when enabled. Avoid creating it active before setting source configuration without checking the resulting source configuration in the running game.

For a bounded experiment, reflection can inspect the existing controller's private `_audioLoadReference` and use its `Asset` as the input clip after loading completes. Resolve the original definition's indexes and volume separately. Keep the original controller and loaded reference alive until the added player has stopped and been destroyed. This is a conditional static inference, not a demonstrated ownership contract: disabling or destroying the controller disposes that reference. Acquiring a separate loaded reference provides a more independent lifetime. `Configure` with nonempty indexes creates split clips owned by the receiving player; with no indexes it uses the supplied clip directly. Do not share the original player's private `_clips` because the original may destroy its owned split clips when disabled or reconfigured. Test load failure, cancellation and teardown.
