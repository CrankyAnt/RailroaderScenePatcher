# Valid crossing detection after a reroute

This investigation serves [Test correction of valid crossing detection after a track reroute](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/issues/23). At the examined Bryson Everett crossing, replacing the three span references in one existing set transferred detection from a still-valid original track to an alternative track in both directions. The original spans remained registered but no longer activated the sampled controller outputs during their passages. The alternative track activated the controller, and the operator reported working gates, lights and bells. Restoring the original references and removing the added spans passed the checks below, followed by a successful original-track control. This is evidence for one crossing, fixture and build, not a universal timing or safety guarantee.

## Source and fixture

The experiment reuses the independently authored fixture and explicit span inputs from [additional detection](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/9495abf1fcef5f3c129a6ebd63a0af8a3c62604a/Research/AdditionalCrossingDetection.md). It adds the straight segment `SCrankyAntDetection21` to `rr/bushnell-whittier`. This is the earlier additional-track fixture, used as an alternative route while the valid old route remains traversable. It is not a newly modelled track reroute. [Equipment research](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/9c0403e487c98a0ac422ed67fa103e19d9e94be3/Research/CrossingEquipment.md) and the [continuation assessment](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/3e5970722bb278cbbaee003300ada6ce916df702/Research/ApplicableOperationsContinuationPlan.md) are established inputs, not evidence that a valid wrong route was corrected.

The installation reports Railroader 0.6.2 experimental build 26335745. The installed `Railroader.dll` SHA-256 is `8E9ACFD583C1DA79655C970773D42F2179E999F62DC36A3C6BC3A7B94C27A91E`; `Assembly-CSharp.dll` is `5E43CE895015E2CEECD28C7994D3055CBE3BD8FB45E37B417D586F129BD1F7AC`. Neither hash identifies all scene assets. The process command line and startup log identified the isolated test home. The disposable save was loaded from its main snapshot and was not manually saved during the trial.

## First bounded replacement and restoration

The controller originally had two sets. Set 0 contained `Pcyi`, `Pipr`, `Pws9` in approach A, island, approach B order. Set 1 contained `Pdlh`, `P6id`, `P1db`. The probe retained both set identities and their order. It created `CrankyAntDetection21_A`, `_I` and `_B` on the alternative segment and replaced only the three members of set 0. The six original spans stayed registered. Sampling continued to check identity, original endpoints, formatted computed points, length and segment IDs, controller references and thresholds, and selected equipment references. Point formatting limits the precision of that comparison. These are the checks implemented by the [trial source](CrossingDetectionProbe/Snapshots/Trial01/DetectionTrial.cs.txt), not an exhaustive account of every indirect reference or cache field.

The input ranges, measured from end A of the fixture segment, were 308 to 550 for approach A, 292 to 308 for the island and 292 to 50 for approach B. The probe reported nonempty computed coverage and exact new graph registrations before changing membership. Its reported lengths were approximately 242, 16 and 242 game units respectively. These ranges are the same declared inputs as the earlier detection experiment.

Static inspection of the identified `Assembly-CSharp.dll` found three span references in `GradeCrossingController.CircuitSet` and no other instance fields in that type. `UpdateCircuit` tests island occupancy and estimates approach arrival times against controller thresholds. The controller loop recomputes the combined state and writes its outputs. This describes the inspected build; it does not prove that other classes have no affected state.

The replacement succeeded with all sampled spans clear and controller outputs false. With a locomotive stopped outside those spans, explicit removal restored the original three members, unregistered the owned spans and requested destruction. A subsequent verify command, issued after removal and after a frame, found the owned root destroyed and rechecked the captured original state. This proves a bounded structural change and restoration in one live world. It does not by itself prove train detection transfer or physical equipment behavior. The loaded trial had no isolated-home mutation guard, and its unload cleanup and `verify` precondition contained defects. The home was independently checked, and explicit removal preceded verification. The [current source](CrossingDetectionProbe/README.md) fixes those defects but is a different, not yet runtime-tested build.

After restoration, a user drove the locomotive through the original set-0 route. Occupancy progressed from `Pws9` through `Pipr` to `Pcyi`, then all six spans cleared. The sampled lights, gates and bell controller values became true and later returned false. The gate observer also recorded arm motion. Controller values alone are not lamp or bell observations. After the train stopped clear, the probe applied the same set-0 replacement again for the changed-membership passages.

On the first changed-membership old-route passage, occupancy progressed from detached `Pcyi` through detached `Pipr` to detached `Pws9`. The reverse passage progressed through the same spans in the opposite order. The replacement spans and the three controller outputs remained false in the recorded half-second samples. The operator reported that the equipment stayed off on the second passage. This demonstrates that the old spans still report occupancy while detached in both directions in this fixture. Sampling alone cannot exclude a shorter output transition.

The first alternative-track passage progressed from replacement approach B through the replacement island to approach A. The reverse passage progressed through the same spans in the opposite order. The six original spans stayed clear in the samples. The sampled controller outputs became true and later false. The gate observer recorded arm motion. The operator reported that the gates, bells and lights operated on both passages. This physical observation is separate from the controller and arm telemetry.

After both alternative-track passages, all nine spans were sampled clear and the outputs were false. The locomotive stopped. Explicit removal restored set 0's original references, unregistered the added spans and requested destruction. A later verification found the owned root destroyed and rechecked original membership, registrations, endpoints, formatted geometry, controller references and thresholds, and selected equipment references. The final restored-route passage progressed from `Pws9` through `Pipr` to `Pcyi`, then all six original spans cleared. The sampled controller outputs activated and released; the operator reported that the gates, bells and lights worked.

## Evidence and limits

The [selected probe evidence](Evidence/Issue23Selected.txt) retains all 0.5-second occupancy and output samples, structural checks and selected gate-observer records. Each `L` number identifies the original local probe-log line. The [exporter](Evidence/ExportCrossingEvidence.ps1) selects 3,807 lines from the 3,908-line source, writes LF line endings and omits train-inspection records. The source SHA-256 is `0C6BF5D0C67AD6D2A9EA9F3D12A9BE0413B5E3CB54AB627766A251FBBA361577`; the selected file SHA-256 is `754EC88424325303BFE67BD92758525B6D7C2479CD0500A943D608041087A495`. The full probe log and game logs remain local. The game logs contain player identifiers and are not included here.

The second replacement begins at source line `L1485`. Representative sampled passage intervals are `L1653` to `L1755` for old route A to B, `L1952` to `L2047` for old route B to A, `L2255` to `L2722` for alternative route B to A, and `L2770` to `L3193` for alternative route A to B. These intervals include stationary samples and are not exact motion boundaries. Final removal appears at `L3276`, deferred destruction verification at `L3351`, and the final restored-route control samples span `L3413` to `L3777`. The operator drove the passages and separately reported that equipment stayed off on the second old-route passage and worked on both alternative-track passages and the final restored control. These reports are observations, not measurements of warning time or evidence that the bell was isolated from other sounds.

The previously reported [first-activation gate anomaly](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/9495abf1fcef5f3c129a6ebd63a0af8a3c62604a/Research/AdditionalCrossingDetection.md) remains a build-specific qualification. The probe samples cannot exclude short transitions between samples. Its gate-pose records do not independently establish audible bell output or lamp illumination. No claim is made about arbitrary geometries, other equipment, scene assets, timing guarantees, cross-version support, automatic application or exhaustive rollback.

The test home returned to its neutral baseline. The game exited without a manual save; its automatic save had changed the disposable archive, which was removed. The retained case-library source archive still matched the initial SHA-256. Active `Mods`, `ModDev` and `Saves` were empty, and the original test-home preferences were restored byte for byte with no enabled mods or client mods. The active probe logged a refused unload cleanup at `L3905` after the map was no longer active. It had already completed explicit removal and deferred-destruction verification at `L3276` and `L3351`. The unload refusal is not evidence of a successful automatic cleanup path.
