# Select targets in the world

Open **Scene patches** in the native map editor. Choose **Select in world** in the command band and click an object marking. The markings use the positions of supported inventory objects, including inactive objects. To check the candidate's scene, path, component, and any identifiers in its binding details, select **Details** in the footer: the message area then shows them below the candidate line, and a second click hides them. **Details** in the footer is greyed out until something was clicked. The footer's first message slot, under **World choice**, gives a short purpose, such as `Pick an object in the world.`, says that native world clicks are suspended until **Cancel** restores them, and shows the candidate as a blue `Candidate: …` line; A click on a `+` selects the object of that `+`. A click inside frames but on no `+` selects the object whose `+` is nearest on screen. The frame and marker a click would select turn blue under the pointer, stay blue after the click and are gold once the object is inspected. Only where two or more `+` markers stand on one spot, closer than half a marker, can a click not tell them apart: the message then starts with `N candidates` and a numbered list opens beside the click. Click the row of the one you want. **Escape** closes only that list; the choice stays open for another click. The row under the pointer is filled blue, and the world draws that row's candidate over the others on its spot, with a heavy frame and a larger, filled marker. A target you confirmed and have not used yet is the one green marking: the track the object is bound to or follows now is not drawn beside it and returns once the target is used or withdrawn. Choose **Confirm** in the footer to select that object for inspection. For an editable object, the fields of **Numeric** mode on the **Placement** tab then show the inspected placement.

You can click the square marking, the drawn object frame, or a displayed identity label. Labels keep a fixed screen text size on their opaque label backing. Markings and labels stay outside native windows and controls. The colors follow the panel: the inspected object gets a gold frame and an `Object: …` label, with a red rule on the label when it has a detected problem; a blue `New: …` marks the candidate of a choice, the candidates of one spot are the numbered rows of the list beside the click, and a green `Confirmed: …` marks a confirmed target while its object is inspected.

Object bounds come from enabled renderers on active objects, except effects such as smoke, water spray or the beam of a lamp, invisible shadow casters and lower levels of detail. A frame surrounds the part of the object at its square marker and the parts next to it; a part far from it, or far larger than it, stays outside the frame. A baked scenery group, such as a station building with its platform and lamps, is one object for everything in it: its frame surrounds all its parts. The frame is one box around those parts, turned with the object, drawn as that box's outline on screen. So it follows an object that lies at an angle to the view instead of boxing it in a screen rectangle, and a click beside the object but inside that rectangle does not pick it. The frame is cut off where the object meets the terrain or the ballast, its wider parts included, and it stays one box: the buried foot of a post is left out, a part that runs into a bank is framed up to the bank, and on sloping ground the bottom of the box lies at the lowest ground under the part, so a heap on a slope keeps a box frame. A bridge over a valley keeps its whole box; the track on its deck and the water under it are no ground. An object with several parts has one frame around them. An object that has nothing of its own to frame, such as a part that is switched off, still shows a small frame around its `+` within 500 metres; the construction scenery of a bridge is framed by what it shows and hides that is no inventory object itself, and otherwise by the small frame around its own `+`, so it is never framed as the bridge is. Close up, the frame always shows, also when the object fills the view. A frame is never smaller than its marker with a small margin, so a small or distant object still shows a frame around its `+`. A label stands centred under its marker and covers no other marker and no native panel; where it would, it stands aside or above. An object farther than 500 metres from the camera shows only its square marker and any label, in the colour its frame would have; you can still click the marker. An inactive object remains selectable through its drawn square marker and identity label. During a replacement choice, when the source object is also offered in an overlap, its label reads `Object / Choice: …` until you choose it.

With a scene object inspected, select **Object**, **Segment** or **Node** in the **Replacement targets** section of the **Connections** tab. The gold frame and the `Object: …` label identify the source object the choice belongs to. For a segment, point at its track in the world. For a node, click its marking. The footer's first message slot shows the candidate as a blue `New: …` under **World choice**, and **Details** shows its identity: the segment ID and both endpoint IDs, or the node ID and map position.

Hovering a track shows the selectable ribbon around its curve, covering the rails and ties with a minimum screen width at a distance. Clicking a shared endpoint or overlapping ribbons offers every matching candidate for an explicit choice.

In a pick mode every candidate of that mode in view carries a `+` marker without the pointer over it; a track carries it within 500 m of the camera. A candidate turns blue under the pointer, in a saturated tint, and stays blue in a lighter tint once you clicked it, until **Confirm**. Green marks what the inspected object is related to now: a confirmed target, the track it follows and, while the **Connections** tab is open, the tracks of its current binding, whether the map or a patch set it. The blue marking labelled `New: …` identifies the candidate under consideration. If the square markers of several candidates stand on one spot at the pointer, a numbered list opens beside the pointer; each row leads with the name that tells its candidate from the others. Click a row to make that object the candidate, or press **Escape** to close the list and click elsewhere. The world stays navigable while the list is open. The frame a click would pick is blue under the pointer and stays blue after the click; **Confirm** makes the candidate the inspected object, framed in gold, or, for a replacement, snap or follow pick, the confirmed target in green. An object shows its frame within 500 metres of the camera and only its square marker beyond. Use **Focus** in the footer to check the location. You can navigate outside the panel and inspect other inventory entries while the choice stays open for its original source.

A game-defined group contains objects held together by a game component. The first supported kind is a grade-crossing setup: its controller and the inventory objects below its root, such as lights, gates, the bell, detection spans and crossing pads. Whistle signs are separate objects at every distance. Grouping affects only how objects appear in the world. Each object keeps its own inventory entry, outliner row and edits; the group has no entry of its own. The controller manages the setup's invisible detection spans, so they are left out of the group's frame and choice list. They remain in the outliner as **Inspection only** rows. To change detection, use the controller's **Connections** tab.

The distance to a group is measured from the camera to the bounds of its visible objects. A grade-crossing setup's detection spans do not count. At 125 m or less, each object has its own square marker and object frame and can be picked individually. Farther away, the group has one square marker and one frame around its visible objects. The frame is a single box, turned with the crossing and cut off at the ground like an object's frame. It appears within 500 m; beyond that, only the square marker remains. A setup with just one inventory object appears as that object. These rules apply to every scene-object choice. If a replacement choice offers only some of the group's objects, the group still appears, but its choice list contains only those objects. If a choice offers only a setup's detection spans, no group appears and the spans are shown individually.

Beyond 125 m, you can pick the group's square marker or frame as you would an object's. The frame turns blue when the pointer is over a place where a click would pick the group. Clicking opens a list beside the pointer with the group's objects, excluding a grade-crossing setup's detection spans. If the group's square marker overlaps other markers at the pointer, those objects appear in the same list. Choose a row to make that object the candidate, then select **Confirm** to inspect it. The candidate has its own blue square marker and frame; the inspected object has its own gold marker and frame. The rest of the group keeps its shared marker and frame. Opening, using or closing the list does not change scene objects, patch data or completed edits. You can still navigate the world while the list is open.

Choose **Confirm** in the footer after checking the candidate. The confirmed identity is recorded in the loaded map context and shown in the footer's first message slot, under **World choice**, as a green `Confirmed: …` while the object it was chosen for is inspected. A choice made by **Select in world** alone, without a source object, does not show it there. If the candidate is missing, ambiguous, or has changed since the pick, select it again after checking the world. If the source or loaded map context changed, cancel and start again.

Confirmation is refused for an object that shares its scene, path and component with another object: a patch names its target by those three and cannot tell the two apart. The refusal names the shared identity. Such an object can be inspected, but it cannot be edited or chosen; pick an object whose path is unique. **Select in world** only inspects what it confirms, so it also confirms an object that shares its hierarchy path with another; such an object then says under **Supported edits** why it cannot be edited. A choice of a replacement object does not offer such an object, because a patch could not name it. A refused confirmation says why in the footer and in the log. Use **Cancel** in the footer to close a pending choice. A pending choice does not change the scene, scene patch file, or completed edit history. Confirmation records a choice for a scene patch editing operation. The [v1 scene patch file reference](scene-patch-file-v1.md) describes the supported patch fields and application behavior.

Confirm or cancel the choice before applying placement or activity changes in **Scene patches**. Completed edits still use the native map editor's **Undo**, **Redo**, and **Save** controls. See [edit and save fixed placement in the native map editor](editing-placement-in-the-map-editor.md).

For a one-time physical placement, use **Track** or **Node** in **Snap** mode on the **Placement** tab. Confirmation prepares the result; **Apply** makes the edit. See [snap an object's position to a track](snapping-to-track.md). The replacement choices above confirm identities without changing logical binding.
