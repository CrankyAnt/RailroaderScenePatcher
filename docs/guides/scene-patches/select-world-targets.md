# Select targets in the world

Open **Scene patches** in the native map editor and click **Select in world** in the command band. Click a marking to choose a supported inventory object, including an inactive one. Each marking is at its object's position. You can click its `+`, its frame or its identity label. If you click inside frames without hitting a `+`, you choose the object whose `+` is nearest on screen. The candidate turns blue under the pointer and stays blue after you click. The blue is more saturated under the pointer and returns to plain blue after a click. Click **Confirm** in the footer to inspect the object. Its frame and marker then turn gold. If the object is editable, **Numeric** mode on the **Placement** tab shows its inspected placement.

The footer's first message slot, **World choice**, tells you what to choose, for example `Choose an object in the world.` The Map Editor can still respond to world clicks while **Select in world** is open. The candidate appears on a blue `Object <n>: …` line. Once you have clicked a candidate, **Details** becomes available in the footer. Click it to show the candidate's scene, path, component and any binding identifiers below that line. Click it again to hide them.

A numbered list opens beside the pointer when two or more `+` markers are closer than half a marker and a click cannot distinguish them. The footer then starts with `N objects. Choose an item from the list next to the pointer.` Select the row you want. **Escape** closes the list and leaves the choice open so you can click elsewhere. The row under the pointer has a blue background. Hover the row or the candidate's `+` to see its blue marking in the world. A track shows a ribbon and an object shows a box.

A confirmed target stays green until you use or withdraw it. While it is green, the track the object is currently bound to or follows is hidden. The track reappears when you use or withdraw the target.

Labels have opaque backgrounds and keep the same text size on screen. Markings and labels stay outside native windows and controls. Their colors match the panel. The inspected object has a gold frame with its square marker at the center and no label, so nothing stands in the way of its gizmo. The panel shows any detected problem. A blue `New: …` label identifies the candidate you are considering. Candidates at the same spot appear as numbered rows in the list beside the pointer. A green `Confirmed: …` label identifies a confirmed target while you inspect the object it was chosen for.

Object bounds come from the object's renderers, except effects such as smoke, water spray or the beam of a lamp, invisible shadow casters and lower levels of detail. A renderer that the game hides at a distance still counts (a static-batched building part once it has been shown with the patcher's markings since the map loaded), so a frame keeps its size and shape as you zoom and while the game culls the object; a renderer or object that is switched off does not count. The square marker stands on the middle of the framed box, a point of the object, so it stays on the object as you zoom and turn the camera. A crossing pad lies on its track and is framed whole, including where its ends meet the road. A frame surrounds the part of the object at its square marker and the parts next to it; a part far from it, or far larger than it, stays outside the frame. A baked scenery group, such as a station building with its platform and lamps, is one object for everything in it: its frame surrounds all its parts. The frame is one box around those parts, turned with the object, drawn as that box's outline on screen. So it follows an object that lies at an angle to the view instead of boxing it in a screen rectangle, and a click beside the object but inside that rectangle does not pick it. The frame is cut off where the object meets the terrain or the ballast, its wider parts included, and it stays one box: the buried foot of a post is left out, a part that runs into a bank is framed up to the bank, and on sloping ground the bottom of the box lies at the lowest ground under the part, so a heap on a slope keeps a box frame. A bridge over a valley keeps its whole box; the track on its deck and the water under it are no ground. An object with several parts has one frame around them. An object that has nothing of its own to frame, such as a part that is switched off, still shows a small frame around its `+` within 500 meters; the construction scenery of a bridge is framed by what it shows and hides that is no inventory object itself, and otherwise by the small frame around its own `+`, so it is never framed as the bridge is. Close up, the frame always shows, also when the object fills the view. A frame is never smaller than its marker with a small margin, so a small or distant object still shows a frame around its `+`. A label stands centered under its marker and covers no other marker and no native panel; where it would, it stands aside or above. An object farther than 500 meters from the camera shows only its square marker and any label, in the color its frame would have; you can still click the marker. The inspected object keeps its gold frame and marker at any distance. An inactive object remains selectable through its drawn square marker and identity label. During a replacement choice, when the source object is also offered in an overlap, its label reads `Object / Choice: …` until you choose it.

On the inspected object's **Connections** tab, click **Object**, **Segment**, or **Node**. For crossing coverage, open a circuit or detection addition and click **Segment** next to the role you want to change. The gold frame identifies the source object the choice belongs to. For a segment, click its track in the world. For a node, click its marking. The footer's first message slot shows the candidate as a blue `New: …` under **World choice**, and **Details** shows its identity: the segment ID and both endpoint IDs, or the node ID and map position.

Hovering a track shows the selectable ribbon around its curve, covering the rails and ties with a minimum screen width at a distance. Clicking a shared endpoint or overlapping ribbons offers every matching candidate for an explicit choice.

In a pick mode every candidate of that mode in view carries a `+` marker without the pointer over it; a track carries it within 500 m of the camera. A candidate turns blue under the pointer, in a more saturated tint, and stays plain blue once clicked, until **Confirm**. When you pick a track location, you can click another point on the current segment. A crossing role's picker excludes that role's current starting segment. Green marks the current binding, every segment covered by the crossing, the track the object follows and a confirmed target. The current binding appears while the **Connections** tab is open, whether it came from the map or a patch. The blue marking labeled `New: …` identifies the candidate under consideration. If the square markers of several candidates stand on one spot at the pointer, a numbered list opens beside the pointer; each row leads with the name that tells its candidate from the others. Click a row to make that object the candidate, or press **Escape** to close the list and click elsewhere. The world stays navigable while the list is open. The frame a click would pick is blue under the pointer and stays plain blue after the click; **Confirm** makes the candidate the inspected object, framed in gold, or, for a replacement, snap or follow pick, the confirmed target in green. An object shows its frame within 500 meters of the camera and only its square marker beyond. Use **Focus** in the footer to check the location. You can navigate outside the panel and inspect other inventory entries while the choice stays open for its original source.

A game-defined group contains objects that the game handles as one unit under one root. A grade-crossing setup is its controller and the inventory objects below its root, such as lights, gates, the bell, detection spans and crossing pads. A baked scenery group, such as Bryson Coal Dealer, is its group root and the parts below it. Only the hierarchy decides: objects that merely stand close together form no group. Whistle signs are separate objects at every distance. Grouping affects only how objects appear in the world. Each object keeps its own inventory entry, outliner row and edits; the group has no entry of its own. The controller manages the setup's invisible detection spans, so they are left out of the group's frame and choice list. They remain in the outliner as **Inspection only** rows. To change detection, use the controller's **Connections** tab.

The distance to a group is measured from the camera to the bounds of its visible objects. A grade-crossing setup's detection spans do not count. At 125 m or less, each object has its own square marker and object frame and can be picked individually. Farther away, the group has one square marker and one frame around its visible objects. The frame is a single box, turned with the crossing or the group root and cut off at the ground like an object's frame. The group's square marker stands on the middle of that box and stays there as you zoom and turn the camera. It appears within 500 m; beyond that, only the square marker remains. A setup with just one inventory object appears as that object. These rules apply to every scene-object choice. If a replacement choice offers only some of the group's objects, the group still appears, but its choice list contains only those objects. If a choice offers only a setup's detection spans, no group appears and the spans are shown individually.

Beyond 125 m, you can pick the group's square marker or frame as you would an object's. The frame turns blue when the pointer is over a place where a click would pick the group. Clicking opens a list beside the pointer with the group's objects, excluding a grade-crossing setup's detection spans. If the group's square marker overlaps other markers at the pointer, those objects appear in the same list. Choose a row to make that object the candidate, then click **Confirm** to inspect it. The candidate has its own blue square marker and frame; the inspected object has its own gold marker and frame. The rest of the group keeps its shared marker and frame. Opening, using or closing the list does not change scene objects, patch data or completed edits. You can still navigate the world while the list is open.

Click **Confirm** in the footer after checking the candidate. The confirmed identity is recorded in the loaded map context and shown in the footer's first message slot, under **World choice**, as a green `Confirmed: …` while the object it was chosen for is inspected. A choice made by **Select in world** alone, without a source object, does not show it there. If the candidate is missing, ambiguous, or has changed since the pick, select it again after checking the world. If the source or loaded map context changed, cancel and start again.

Confirmation is refused for an object that shares its scene, path and component with another object: a patch names its target by those three and cannot tell the two apart. The refusal names the shared identity. Such an object can be inspected, but it cannot be edited or chosen; pick an object whose path is unique. **Select in world** only inspects what it confirms, so it also confirms an object that shares its hierarchy path with another; such an object then says under **Supported edits** why it cannot be edited. A choice of a replacement object does not offer such an object, because a patch could not name it. A refused confirmation says why in the footer and in the log. Use **Cancel** in the footer to close a pending choice. A pending choice does not change the scene, scene patch file, or completed edit history. Confirmation records a choice for a scene patch editing operation. The [v1 scene patch file reference](scene-patch-file-v1.md) describes the supported patch fields and application behavior.

Confirm or cancel the choice before applying placement or activity changes in **Scene patches**. Completed edits still use the native map editor's **Undo**, **Redo**, and **Save** controls. See [edit and save fixed placement in the native map editor](editing-placement-in-the-map-editor.md).

For a one-time physical placement, use **Track** or **Node** in **Snap** mode on the **Placement** tab. Confirmation prepares the result; **Apply** makes the edit. See [snap an object's position to a track](snapping-to-track.md). Editing actions in **Placement** and **Connections** pause native Map Editor world clicks, including right-click. You can still rotate and pan the camera with the right and middle mouse buttons. You can still use **Scene patches**, its world choices and its gizmo handles. **Apply**, **Discard**, **Start**, **Set**, **Confirm** or **Cancel** ends the block at the end of the corresponding action; **Read**, or leaving a **Numeric** field without a change, ends it too. In **Gizmo** mode it ends when you switch mode or tab, clear the inspection or close the window.
