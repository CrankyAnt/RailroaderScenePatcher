# Inspect scene objects

Open **Scene patches** from the **Scene Patcher** icon in the top-right icon strip of the native map editor. The icon appears only in the native map editor. If the strip cannot take it, a floating **Scene Patcher** button replaces it, and the reason appears under **Diagnostics** in the session details. The window opens flush with the left screen edge, directly below the native Map Editor window. It is 734 units wide by default, or as wide as the Map Editor window when that is wider, and it extends down to the bottom of the screen. Where fewer than 600 units of height remain there, as on a 1080-high screen, it opens at the top right of the screen instead, below the button strip and the status readout, with the same width. Drag its title to move it, and use its close control or the entry to close it. Closing cancels a pending world choice. After that it stays where you put it, at the size you gave it, like a native window: a native window or menu that opens over it does not move or resize it. Only a screen or UI-scale change that leaves it partly off screen places it again. The title bar holds three glyphs left of the close control, each named in its tooltip: the **?** for help on the whole window, **Reset layout** (a round arrow), which puts the window back at that default place and restores the outliner width, and **Session details** (a page), which opens and closes the session details in a window of their own: the editing document, the supplied definitions, diagnostics and, during a world choice, its candidates. That window can be moved and resized, stays where you put it, and closes with the panel. Its minimum size is 704 by 470 units, which keeps the outliner, the command band and a tab body in view; in a low window the message area gives way first and keeps one message in view. A panel that was open when you left the native map editor opens again when you enter it. Select **Refresh** in the command band. Refresh reads the loaded scene and the edited mod's supplied definitions and verifies stored target identities. It does not apply patches, save files, change object activity, repair bindings or regenerate dependent output. Objects need no patch or warning to appear.

The command band across the top is split at the divider between the two columns. Above the outliner it holds **Refresh** and two status lamps: a dot with the word **Current** or **Stale**, and a dot with **Saved** or **Unsaved**. A lamp is green when all is well and amber otherwise; it is information, not a button, and its tooltip gives the reason. Above the inspector the band holds **Select in world**, **Focus**, **Clear** and **Copy ID**. **Clear** ends the inspection and cancels a pending world choice, gizmo or snap; what you changed stays. A click on the inspected row, or Escape with the pointer over the window, does the same. With the pointer over the world, Escape cancels only a pending world choice or an active gizmo. A command that cannot run now stays in place, greyed out: **Select in world** while a world choice is pending, **Focus** and **Copy ID** while nothing is inspected, **Copy ID** also while the edited mod's patch document cannot be read, **Clear** while nothing is inspected and no world choice is pending. Its tooltip keeps its description and adds the reason after **Unavailable:**, as one plain sentence without a label of its own. Every **?** opens its help in a separate window beside the panel, at the height of that **?**: an introduction, short explanations and links to the guides. There is one help window at a time; it never stands over the panel, and the help of a tab closes when you open another tab. The help covers nothing of the panel's own content; close it with the **?**, which shows **x** while its help is open, or with the help window's own close control. Below the band, the outliner on the left holds a search field with a **?** for outliner help, the **All**, **Changed** (objects the edited mod changes), **Disabled** (objects that are switched off themselves) and **Problems** (objects with a detected problem) filters, a count line in the form `N of M objects`, and the object rows. **Fit**, at the right end of the outliner's top row beside the divider, sizes the outliner to most of the names, and the divider between the outliner and the inspector can be dragged. When the column would need more than 40 percent of the window, **Fit** stops there and a message with the source **Fit** says how wide the names are. Three notes at the bottom of the outliner state that Undo, Redo and Save are the Map Editor's own, when to refresh after native edits, and that a reload clears the Undo history. An object opens on its own placement mode: **Follow** for an object that follows a track, **Numeric** otherwise. The inspector on the right shows the object's full name while its row has room, a state line (family, **Inactive**, `* CHANGED` and the problem count), and four tabs: **Object**, **Placement**, **Connections** and **Changes**. Each tab body starts with a one-line description of the tab and a **?** at the right end of that row, which opens the tab's help in the help window. The actions of a tab stand in one row at the bottom of that tab, below its scrolling content. The footer under the tabs is the same on every tab: a message area above one row with **Remove modification** and **Recheck**, which act on the inspected object whatever tab is open. The first message says what the window is waiting for, such as a world choice, a gizmo or a snap, or why the open tab cannot edit the object now. Below it stands the result or refusal of the last action, with the action group it came from as its bold source, and above that a consequence that arrived by itself afterwards, such as terrain output. A result goes when it no longer describes what you see: on a new action, on native Undo or Redo, and when another object is inspected. The result of a tab's own action also goes when you change the tab or the placement mode; a result of **Remove modification** or **Recheck** stays through that. While a world choice is pending, the footer's row holds **Confirm**, **Cancel**, **Focus** and **Details**, and the controls of the tab are greyed out until the choice ends; while a gizmo is active the row holds its **Cancel**. Each tab body scrolls on its own; the window itself does not.

A detected problem follows what the game does with an object. An object that is switched off itself is never listed with a problem of any kind: someone switched it off, and the game ignores it until someone enables it. Find such objects under **Disabled**. An object that only a culler hides while the camera is far away is not switched off. Enabled, the kinds differ:

- A crossing pad needs its binding: an empty one, or one that names a segment that no longer exists, is a problem.
- A starter placement marker needs its binding whether it is enabled or not, because a new-game setup reads it either way: an empty binding, or one that names a segment that no longer exists, is a problem. This is the one exception to the rule above; see [starter placement markers](#starter-placement-markers).
- A yard limit, speed, station or whistle sign, a milepost, a marker under `Setups` that no setup holds and any other Generic marker need no binding. An empty binding is shown as a **Marker binding note** on the **Connections** tab, not as a problem. A stored segment that no longer exists is a problem while the object is enabled, and [repairing the binding](repairing-track-bindings.md) corrects it.
- An independent board has no track binding and no binding problem.

A problem is a defect you can repair with the patcher, such as a broken binding. Three other things are shown and are not problems:

- Why an object of a supported family cannot be edited: **Supported edits** on the **Object** tab reads **Not editable:** with the reason. The object stays in its family's folder. One such reason is a hierarchy path that two objects share: a patch names its target by scene, path and component, so it cannot name one of them. Each of the two rows can still be inspected.
- An object of a kind the patcher has no edit for yet and the Map Editor does not edit. Such objects stand in one **Inspection only** group at the end of the outliner, after the family folders, each row with its family behind its name. The group is there only while it holds objects. Its rows have no problem marker and no edit; search and the **All** and **Disabled** filters include them, and the **Object** tab starts with the reason the kind is not yet supported. Mileposts, the track spans of grade crossings, crossing lights, gates and bells, storage piles of industries, markers of another type than Generic, the crossbucks of unprotected crossings and baked scenery groups that hold game logic, terrain shaping or track markers are in this group in the current version.
- What the inspection could not read or check, such as an incomplete reference scan or a segment that could not be checked: **Inspection notes** on the **Object** tab, without a problem marker, because nothing there can be repaired with the patcher.

Some scene objects are not listed at all: everything below the `CTC`, `CTC Auxiliary`, `Setups`, `Ops`, `MapFeatures`, `Progressions`, `MapManager` and `Editor Directional Light` scene roots apart from starter placement markers, signal markers anywhere, starter placement markers that no active setup uses, the map objects whose track anchor the native Map Editor edits (water, coal and diesel facilities) and other objects of the map's `Map.json`, track spans the game builds from the map's operations data, map labels, objects the game creates while it runs, the builder photo rig, and objects without a renderer, collider or light that no family covers.

Use [world selection](select-world-targets.md) to find an object by its marking or check a replacement candidate against the track and scene.

## Find and inspect an object

Search by name, full hierarchy path, family, binding identifier, dependency or detected problem. The inventory includes active and inactive loaded objects. Rows sit under a folder for their family, in the order of their names, which stays collapsed until you search or filter; the folder of a newly inspected object opens once. Select the object's row in the outliner. Markers stand before its name: a red `!` marks a detected problem, an amber `*` an object this mod changes, and a green `+` an object it adds. The native Map Editor puts `*` and `+` after a name; here they come first, so that a long name that is cut off keeps them. The state line under the object's name shows the same as the badges `* CHANGED` and `+ ADDED`. Neither marker means unsaved work: only the **Unsaved** lamp does. `* CHANGED` means that this mod's patch document changes the object. An edit whose value equals the object's value without this mod takes that setting out of the modification, so enabling an object you disabled leaves it unchanged again; an edit control is inactive while its value is the object's current one. A family shows subgroups only where the game itself tells its objects apart: track markers of more than one marker type stand under their types, and never under a name. An inactive object's name is italic and followed by `inactive`. The row's tooltip gives the name, path, family and problems. The **Object** tab shows the identity (scene, component, structure and path), whether the object is **Enabled**, its supported edits, its detected problems and its dependencies. The **Placement** tab shows the position in map coordinates and the rotation in degrees, in its **Numeric** mode: six fields for an object that can be edited, two running lines of text otherwise. The **Connections** tab shows the track binding and the observed bindings. **Copy ID** in the command band copies JSON values from that observation; the footer's message slots and the log then name the object whose identity was copied. A copied identity does not establish support for editing the object.

An independent object has no invented track binding. A missing or empty segment reference appears as a problem. A reference to an existing segment can still name the wrong track. The patcher cannot decide whether a valid binding or physical placement matches the map author's intention. Compare the displayed identifiers and placement with the world.

Supplied definitions remain visible under **Supplied definitions** in the session details when their target is missing or ambiguous, their modification dependency is missing, or their document cannot be read. Such a target cannot be selected. The referring definition identifies the problem. Inspection does not rewrite an unreadable or newer-format document.

## Starter placement markers

A new company game starts with cars that a setup places on the track. Each car placement of the setup reads one marker, its starter placement marker, for its track location: the front of the cut stands at that point and the rest of the cut extends towards the named end of the segment. The marker's own object position plays no part, and can stand hundreds of metres from where the cars appear.

The outliner lists these markers in their own **Starter placement** family, once each:

- A marker that a car placement of an active setup holds is listed, enabled or not, because the game reads it at a new game.
- A marker that only setups that are switched off hold is not listed: the game never reads it.
- A marker that no setup holds is no starter placement marker. It stays an ordinary marker in the **Track marker** family, whatever its name or place under `Setups`.

The **Object** tab lists under **Read by** every setup and car placement that reads the marker, with the cars of each cut and whether the placement is a wreck. A listed marker needs a usable binding whether it is enabled or not: an empty binding, or a stored track location whose segment no longer exists, is a problem. A binding on an existing segment shows no problem.

While a starter placement marker is inspected, the world marks its track point in gold, on the track, with the label **Front of the car cut**, and the track the cut stands on as a gold ribbon from that point back, so the direction the cut extends shows. When the cut's length cannot be read, only the point shows, labelled **Track point**. The **Placement** tab shows that point in map coordinates under **Track point**. **Focus** moves the camera to the track point. When the marker's track location does not resolve on the loaded track, **Focus** is greyed out and its tooltip gives the reason. A stored distance beyond the end of its segment has no track point either: **Focus** is greyed out, and the marker shows no problem, because the binding check only asks whether the segment exists. If the new-game setups cannot be read, no marker is listed as a starter placement marker; every marker then shows an inspection note that says so, and **Diagnostics** in the session details reports it after **Refresh**.

Place the marker on the track on the **Connections** tab, as described in [place a starter placement marker on the track](placing-starter-markers.md). Editing the wreck setting is not available in this version. The marker object's placement, gizmo and activity controls stay greyed out, because the object's placement and activity do not change where the cars start; their tooltips say so.

## Focus and refresh

Select **Focus** in the command band to jump the camera to the inspected object's current position in map coordinates. For an object the game does not move with its floating origin, such as a marker under `Setups` that no setup holds, that is its position in the scene. A track span is its track locations: Focus lands midway between them, and is refused with its reason when neither resolves. A starter placement marker is its track point, not its object position; see [starter placement markers](#starter-placement-markers). Focus keeps the inspected selection, works while the inventory is stale and does not make a scene edit. If the object no longer exists, the message area reports the refusal under **Focus**. Native camera jumps may shift the floating origin or load terrain. Map coordinates remain the placement reference.

Mouse actions inside the product button or panel belong to that UI. Scrolling an observation should not also zoom the world, and dragging the scrollbar or title should not select objects behind the panel. A drag that begins inside remains captured until release. Outside the panel, normal world navigation remains available.

When the inventory is unscanned or changed, the first status lamp turns amber and reads **Stale** in place of **Current**. The lamp also reads **Stale** when the edited mod's patch document was changed outside scene patch editing while the native map editor runs; saving from the native map editor does not make it stale, and neither does a file whose members were only reordered or re-spaced, because its content is equal. Its tooltip and the session details name the reason: the first observation that changed or disappeared, a changed number of observations, a changed map context, or a check that failed. The log receives the same reason. Stale means that the observations differ from the scene or that the check could not complete; using the panel does not make the inventory stale by itself. While the inventory is stale, rows can still be inspected and **Focus** works; only edits are refused. Runtime references, which are held in fields the game does not serialize, such as culling or audio lists, and enabled flags that the game switches while it runs, such as a crossing bell's audio player, appear under **Runtime state** on the **Object** tab and do not make the inventory stale. While the panel is open it compares the scene with the snapshot in the background, a small part in each frame, and starts again two seconds after a comparison ends. A scene change therefore appears as stale after a delay that grows with the number of loaded objects. Supplied-document changes are checked every two seconds. These checks do not replace the displayed snapshot. An edit does not rely on the background comparison: at the moment of the edit the patcher compares the whole scene again and refuses when anything differs. Refresh explicitly replaces the observations and retains the inspected object when its handle and component still exist. Refresh after native edits, Undo/Redo, activity changes or document changes. Supplied document observations update only on explicit refresh.

Focus uses the inspected object itself, not a fresh inventory, so it never jumps to another object that has the same path. Before a complete-document placement, the engine repeats unique resolution and preflight immediately before mutation and checks that the map context has not changed. Scene patch editing integrations can additionally pass an inspected-target token to reject changes in observed placement, bindings or dependencies. Refresh cannot substitute another object with the same path for a completed edit.

## Coverage and dependencies

| Family or ownership path | Inventory coverage and editing route |
| --- | --- |
| Track markers, including yard-limit and station signs | Typed markers and serialized track-relative placement. The object's label does not establish its marker type. Generic markers that match the supported pattern carry the structure `track-marker-v1` and accept binding edits, as described in [repair, replace or detach a track-marker binding](repairing-track-bindings.md); the others show the reason in their problems. A marker that holds a duplicate marker id starts its dependencies with that marker id, followed by every holder with its activity and the registration it has under the marker id; see [markers with a duplicate marker id](repairing-track-bindings.md#markers-with-a-duplicate-marker-id). |
| Mileposts | Marker binding, prefix, origin, mileage and component dependencies. Inspection leaves models and mileage unchanged. |
| Track-group-linked visuals | Serialized `TrackRelativePosition` targets, offsets, terrain snapping and group-driven activity settings. Construction-controlled targets retain their separate segment and target relationships. |
| Crossing pads | Markers with the curve-mesh component, including their generated-geometry dependency. Pads that match the supported pattern carry the structure `crossing-pad-v1` and accept binding edits that regenerate the pad, as described in [rebind a crossing pad and regenerate its geometry](rebinding-crossing-pads.md). |
| Crossing detection | Loaded spans and controller circuit roles. A missing endpoint segment differs from a valid but unintended track. Crossing controllers that match the supported pattern carry the structure `crossing-coverage-v1` and accept coverage edits, as described in [correct the detection coverage of an existing crossing](correcting-crossing-coverage.md); spans the patcher owns appear under `CrankyAnt Scene Patcher crossing coverage`. |
| Existing crossing equipment | Controller-bound lamp-material, gate-animation and bell-player components under loaded crossing controllers. Component presence does not prove functional detection, light output or audible playback. |
| Independent boards | Loaded objects with a renderer culler and a circle map mask and no track marker. Those that match the board pattern accept fixed placement and activity, as described in [move a board with its terrain effects](moving-boards.md); the others show the reason in their problems. |
| Baked scenery groups | Group roots below `World` that the scene saves, that no other family lists and that hold a renderer, collider or light. Groups without game logic or terrain shaping carry the structure `baked-scenery-group-v1` and accept fixed placement and activity of the whole group, as described in [move or disable a baked scenery group](moving-baked-scenery-groups.md); the others stand in the **Inspection only** group with the reason. |
| Retained bridges and construction scenery | The original inventory's identified Ela bridge culler and separate `Game.Mow.TrackConstructionScenery` controller. The controller exposes its segment IDs, construction conditions and referenced targets, including the bridge's steel and abutments. Moving the controller does not imply moving its targets. |
| Generated track trestles | The two `AutoTrestle` controls identified near Ela belong to generated track-segment hierarchies. They remain native track geometry, separately owned from the retained bridge. |
| Industry/passenger spans and map-object anchors | OpsDTO spans and MapObjectSpawner anchors use native map/operations data. This covers the original P7ec/Pfaj, Wilmot and Sox anchor cases. The current Ela passenger span is native operations data; the historical FUSE warning concerns the previous game line. |
| Player-created signs | Use the native sign editor. Its ownership and persistence do not establish an inherited scene-sign editing route. |
| Missing object configurations or industry definitions | The original Sylva diesel-stand and Bryson textile cases concern native configuration/definition data, with no identified scene-object repair. |
| Signals, CTC and interlocking | Their relationships appeared in the historical inventory but the first-version operation boundary excludes their edits. A span's presence in the inventory does not enable these operations. |

Discovery reads loaded objects, including inactive ones. It does not load otherwise absent scenes. Dependencies include child components, serialized marker/span/controller relationships and direct references from loaded scene component fields and lists. Identifier-based or indirect consumers can remain unknown. An unavailable field or graph reports an inspection limit rather than proving a binding valid. Names and proximity never create a functional relationship.

All newly inventoried patterns are inspection-only unless an operation adapter explicitly supports them. The table accounts for the applicable families and ownership cases in the original inventory. It does not promise discovery of arbitrary custom scene hierarchies, require a separate bridge model whitelist, or establish native rendering or gameplay behavior. An unidentified timber object in an earlier bridge image remains unattributed; the two sampled native trestles do not identify that object.

The [candidate ownership findings](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/issues/20#issuecomment-6717432391) distinguish retained signs from native passenger and anchor data. The [operation investigation](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/issues/18#issuecomment-6725711245) records dependency observations and their limits. These research results do not establish production inspection behavior.

The original [candidate families](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/4c00445b048adccb6920c238116d1f7e1e915db9/Research/Scope/CandidateFamilies.md) and [ownership audit](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/f8b3a72f00dc6556e5d37894605b0312658ad598/Research/NativeCapabilityAudit.md) identify the retained bridge family and its native-data distinctions. The later [bridge trial](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/5e99c72dee551762316c0b8cf5aafec5bf40b748/Research/SceneOperationBatch/Evidence/Boot02/Bridge/Report.md) identifies the construction controller's steel and abutment references. The [trestle observations](https://github.com/CrankyAnt/RailroaderScenePatcher/blob/5e99c72dee551762316c0b8cf5aafec5bf40b748/Research/SceneOperationBatch/Evidence/Boot03/Bridge/Report.md) distinguish the sampled track-owned controls from the retained root and the unattributed timber image.
